Predicting Reactions Before the Lab Requires Fast and Accurate Methods

Imagine being able to predict how molecules interact and react before ever stepping into a laboratory. Such a capability could transform how we discover new materials, design molecules, and develop advanced manufacturing processes. Rather than relying on costly and time-consuming trial and error, scientists could explore thousands of chemical reactions computationally, rapidly identifying promising molecules and processes for experimental validation.

Computational methods offer a path toward this vision, but a fundamental challenge remains: balancing accuracy and speed. Widely used methods like density functional theory (DFT) are computationally efficient but often struggle to predict chemical reactions accurately. More accurate approaches, such as coupled-cluster theory, come at a much greater computational cost and remain impractical at scale. This trade-off limits our ability to reliably predict and explore new chemistry at scale.

The problem

Develop a more accurate exchange-correlation functional that improves DFT predictions without sacrificing computational efficiency.

Submissions

We evaluate submissions on a hidden benchmark of reference-quality molecular reaction energies, activation barriers, binding energies, and bond dissociation energies representative of atomic layer deposition (ALD) and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) precursor chemistry.

Submissions should include:

  • Method: A description of the proposed computational approach and its underlying scientific principles.
  • Implementation: Working code and instructions sufficient to independently run and evaluate the method.
  • Results: Evidence of predictive accuracy and computational efficiency on the public benchmark (GMTKN55).

Evaluation considers:

  • Predictive accuracy: Error against the hidden benchmark.
  • Speed and computational effort: Compute time per molecule.
  • Reproducibility: Sufficient documentation to verify the reported results.
