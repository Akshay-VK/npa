# Dataset landscape

Every relevant public dataset for atomistic modeling, with size, elements,
level of theory, and fitness for this project. The landscape is divided into
four categories: equilibrium molecular, reactive, materials, and large-scale
foundation. The purpose is to answer: where can we train, and where are the
gaps?

**Bottom line**

- **Transition1x** is the only public reactive-dataset, but it is path data
  (NEB images), not trajectories. It covers H/C/N/O, ≤7 heavy atoms, ωB97x/6-31G(d).
- **OMol25** is the largest equilibrium dataset: >100M DFT calculations at
  ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD, 83 elements, ≤350 atoms, 83M unique systems. It is the
  primary source for universal-potential pre-training.
- **OMat24 / OC20 / OC22** are inorganic materials datasets, PBE-level.
- **QM9 / QM7 / QM8** are small organic datasets, B3LYP/ωB97 level, very
  limited size. Not useful as primary training data.
- There is **no public reactive-dynamics dataset** at scale. This is the
  project's biggest data gap.
- The accuracy ceiling is set by the label level: OMol25 = ωB97M-V (3.81
  kcal/mol on the challenge), not CCSD(T). Multi-fidelity is required to
  surpass that ceiling.

## Equilibrium molecular datasets

| Dataset | Size | Elements | Level of theory | Unique systems | Fitness for project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OMol25** | >100M DFT calculations | 83 | ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD | ~83M | **Primary pre-training source.** Caps accuracy at ωB97M-V level. |
| **QM9** | 134k | H, C, N, O, F | B3LYP | 134k | Not useful for training; useful for quick sanity check. |
| **QM7** | 7k | H, C, N, O | B3LYP | 7k | Not useful. |
| **QM8** | 22k | H, C, N, O | B3LYP | 22k | Not useful. |
| **SPICE** | ~1M | H, C, N, O, S, P, halogens | ωB97M-D3(BJ) | ~1M | Can be used as auxiliary data for non-covalent interactions. |
| **Aniso-1x / ANI-1ccx** | ~2M / ~1.5M | H, C, N, O, F | B3LYP/cc-pVTZ (rederived) | — | Transfer-learned ANI; good for fine-tuning on organic thermochemistry. |

**OMol25** is the critical dataset. Its properties are verified from the
arXiv abstract (retrieved 2026-11-07):

- >100 million DFT calculations
- ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD level of theory
- 83 elements (most of the periodic table)
- Systems up to 350 atoms
- ~83 million unique systems
- Includes reactive structures, variable charge/spin, explicit solvation

The dataset's accuracy ceiling must be stated: because the labels are ωB97M-V,
any model trained *only* on OMol25 cannot credibly claim to beat ωB97M-V on
the same observables. See [the verification log](../09-sources/02-verification-log.md) for the inference about the leaderboard.

> **In plain words.** The biggest dataset was labelled by a method that is good
> but not perfect. Training on it will reproduce that method's accuracy, not
> surpass it. To go beyond, you need a smaller, more expensive pile of better
> labels.

## Reactive datasets

| Dataset | Size | Reactions | Level of theory | Elements | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Transition1x** | 9.6M configurations | ~10k reactions | ωB97x/6-31G(d) | H, C, N, O | NEB path images (not dynamics) |
| **NN-EVB (historical)** | small | small | various | various | Empirical valence bond; not public. |
| **DIFFER** | unverified | unverified | unverified | unverified | Diffusion-based TS generation; not verified this pass. |
| **Medea** | unverified | unverified | unverified | unverified | Recent TS dataset; not verified this pass. |

**Transition1x** is the only public reactive dataset. Its properties are
verified (retrieved 2026-11-07):

- 9.6 million DFT calculations
- ~10,000 reactions
- ωB97x/6-31G(d) level of theory
- Up to 7 heavy atoms (C, N, O), with up to 6 bond changes
- Generated by Nudged Elastic Band, saving intermediate images
- Abstract states: "ML models cannot learn features in transition-state regions
  solely by training on hitherto popular benchmark datasets."

**The critical caveat.** Transition1x contains *path images*, not trajectories.
There are no velocities, no time coordinate, and no thermal fluctuations. The
parameter is an arc length along the minimum-energy path, not femtoseconds.
This is the dominant fact: **Transition1x teaches the model where the saddle
is, not how to cross it**.

> **In plain words.** The dataset shows the easiest route over the mountain,
> surveyed at absolute zero, with no weather. Real reactions happen in a storm,
> and they sometimes cross somewhere else.

> **In plain words.** The dataset is photographs of the saddle, not videos of
> the crossing. It teaches the model the location of the hill, not how to climb
> it.

## Materials datasets

| Dataset | Size | Elements | Level of theory | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OMat24** | ~110M | Mixed (mostly metals) | PBE/PBE+U | Inorganic crystals, surfaces |
| **OC20** | ~1M | Mixed | RPBE / PBE | Catalysis, gas-surface reactions |
| **OC22** | ~0.5M | Mixed | RPBE / PBE | Extended catalysis |
| **Alexandria** | ~50M | Mixed | PBE | Periodic materials |

These are not directly fitness for organic reaction chemistry, but they are
the pre-training source for universal models (UMA, MACE-MP). A project that
wants a universal potential should be aware of them.

## The gap: no reactive-dynamics dataset

There is no public dataset of time-resolved reactive trajectories with forces
at scale. The reasons:

- Generating such data requires AIMD or constrained MD at high temperature,
  which is expensive (DFT cost per step).
- The events are rare: a 25 kcal/mol barrier at 300 K gives ∼10⁻⁵ s⁻¹.
- No standard format exists; each group uses its own trajectory file.

**The project's options.**

1. **Use OMol25 + Transition1x as pre-training, then fine-tune on a small
   reactive dataset.** This is the multi-fidelity approach (see
   [label fidelity](../04-data/03-label-fidelity-and-multifidelity.md)).

2. **Generate a small reactive dataset using AIMD or scripted reactions.**
   Aim for 100–1000 trajectories with barriers, at a level of theory the
   project can afford (e.g. ωB97x/def2-SVP). This would be a valuable
   resource in its own right.

3. **Do not train on reactive data, and state the limitation explicitly.**
   The model will be accurate on equilibrium chemistry but will not capture
   barrier-crossing. This is an honest result, and the project should say so.

> **In plain words.** There is no standard exam for "can your model predict
> how fast a reaction goes?". Making that exam might be more useful than being
> the first to pass it.

## Fitness summary

| Goal | Best dataset | Alternative | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-train a universal model | OMol25 | OMat24, OC20 | Accuracy capped at ωB97M-V |
| Learn barrier physics | Transition1x (path data only) | None public | No trajectory data |
| Equilibrium thermochemistry | OMol25, ANI-1ccx | QM9, SPICE | None significant |
| Materials modeling | OMat24, MACE-MP-0 | OMol25 | Different chemistry domain |

## What this means for the project

1. **OMol25 is the primary pre-training source.** It is large, diverse, and
   at the right level of theory for the challenge. But the accuracy ceiling
   is ωB97M-V. Plan for multi-fidelity if the goal is to surpass it.

2. **Transition1x is the only reactive data, but it is path data, not
   trajectories.** Use it to teach the model where saddles are, but do not
   expect it to teach dynamics.

3. **If the project needs reactive trajectories, plan to generate a small
   AIMD dataset in phase 3 or 4.** Do not wait for a public dataset that does
   not exist.

4. **Benchmark on equilibrium data first.** It is cheaper and the model must
   get this right before it can hope to get dynamics right.

## Open questions

- Is there a public reactive-trajectory dataset that this search missed? If
  so, it changes the data plan entirely.
- How well does a model trained on OMol25 + fine-tuned on Transition1x
  generalize to barrier heights it has not seen?
- What is the minimum viable reactive dataset size? 10 trajectories? 100?
  1000? This determines whether generating the data is a weeks-long or
  months-long effort.

## What would change this document

- Release of a large reactive-trajectory dataset (AIMD or enhanced sampling).
- A published demonstration that path-trained models transfer to trajectory
  accuracy.
- A decision to generate the project's own small reactive dataset.
